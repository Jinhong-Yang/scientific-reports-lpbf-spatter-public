## Editorial standard

Scientific Reports states that peer review assesses the technical soundness
and scientific validity of methods, analysis, and interpretation, including
whether they are properly conducted and supported by the data. The study is
therefore optimized for traceability and validity, not for manufacturing a
positive result. Source: [Scientific Reports editorial process](https://www.nature.com/srep/about/editorial-process).

The journal's Editorial Board guidance explicitly permits technically sound
negative results and scientifically justified replication studies. Source:
[Scientific Reports Editorial Board responsibilities (PDF)](https://www.nature.com/documents/SREB.pdf).

## Article preparation

The current submission guide describes the following principal constraints:

- Article and Registered Report are the supported research formats; this work
  is planned as an Article.
- The journal recommends, but does not describe as universally mandatory, no
  more than 11 typeset pages and 4,500 main-text words, excluding Abstract,
  Methods, References, and figure legends.
- The title should be no more than 20 words and communicate one scientifically
  accurate message.
- The abstract should be unstructured, contain no references or figures, and
  be no more than 200 words.
- Up to six keywords/key phrases are allowed.
- A conventional Introduction–Results–Discussion–Methods organization is
  suitable.
- Author contributions, a Data Availability statement, and a Competing
  Interests statement are required sections.
- Because custom code is central to the conclusions, the Methods must also
  contain a separately headed Code availability statement describing access
  and any restrictions. The archived version must remain identifiable.
- Display items are limited to eight figures and/or tables; figure legends are
  limited to 350 words each.
- A cover letter and high-resolution figure files are required. A first
  submission may use Word, LaTeX, or PDF; Springer Nature's LaTeX template is
  encouraged.
- The cover letter must give the corresponding author's affiliation and
  contact information, explain journal fit, provide any suggested or excluded
  referees, and state whether there were prior discussions with a Scientific
  Reports Editorial Board Member. Unknown author-only facts must remain
  explicit placeholders rather than being inferred.
- Supplementary Information should be supplied as one separate file,
  preferably PDF, with individual files kept at or below 50 MB.
- Tables must remain editable; statistical tables must state error analysis
  and ranges in their legends.
- LLMs do not meet authorship criteria. Any reportable LLM use must follow the
  journal's current AI-disclosure policy and be documented in the Methods or
  another suitable section.

Source: [Scientific Reports submission guidelines](https://www.nature.com/srep/author-instructions/submission-guidelines).

## Data, materials, and author responsibility

Scientific Reports expects original data underlying a submission to be
preserved and retrievable for reanalysis. It strongly encourages datasets on
which conclusions rely to be available to readers, preferably in public
repositories for large datasets. Access restrictions must be described rather
than hidden. Authors must approve the submitted version and remain accountable
for accuracy and integrity. Source: [Scientific Reports editorial and
publishing policies](https://www.nature.com/srep/journal-policies/editorial-policies).

For this project, public release of AI-Hub-derived raw images is not assumed.
The release plan must separate licensed/raw data from shareable manifests,
code, configurations, predictions, aggregate results, and instructions for
authorized reconstruction.

## Initial-submission checklist verification

The journal's three-page initial-submission checklist was retrieved and
rechecked on 2026-09-15. It requires one manuscript file; main tables embedded
in that file in editable form; Supplementary Information uploaded separately,
preferably as one file; exact title and abstract agreement with the submission
system; corresponding-author identification and email; numbered affiliations
including countries; an unstructured citation-free abstract; no footnotes or
tracked changes; reproducible Methods; author contributions and competing
interests after the references; main figure and table legends after the
references; and a Data Availability section before the references. The title
and author list must also appear on the first page of the Supplementary
Information. Human- and animal-study provisions are not applicable to this
image-computing study, but the authors must select the truthful portal options.
Source: [Scientific Reports checklist for initial submissions](https://www.nature.com/documents/srep-checklist-for-initial-submissions.pdf).

## Internal review threshold

The final self-review uses the journal's referee questions: technical
soundness, evidential support for every claim, statistical soundness, data
availability, literature context, clarity, and relevant ethical concerns. The
project's internal “minor revision” threshold means that no unresolved finding
requires a new core experiment, a change to the primary estimand, recovery of
missing supporting data, or removal of a central unsupported claim. Remaining
items may be limited to localized clarification, presentation, or author-only
metadata. This is an internal readiness classification and not a prediction of
the editor's decision. Source: [Scientific Reports guide to referees](https://www.nature.com/srep/guide-to-referees).
